Version 1 raised false "two transmitters" alarms on clients within a minute. Clients re-adjust their carrier pre-correction in steps of up to 1.1 ppm; APs don't. Version 2 changes four things:

### Live forged-deauth attack test (5 November)

First validation on a transmitted attack. A Heltec ESP32-S3 (new `tools/esp32_deauth_attacker/`, a bounded manual deauth source) sent 60 forged deauths spoofing a phone hotspot we own ("Redmi 9", ch13) while the X310 monitored receive-only. The monitor raised `rf_forged_disconnect` at High/High: 35 of 35 received deauths flagged, forged CFO −0.515 ppm vs the genuine AP's −2.376 ppm (gap 1.86 ppm), naming the impersonated BSSID. Recording in `data/rf_fingerprint/2026-11-05/`. Notes: raw deauth TX on the ESP32 needs the `ieee80211_raw_frame_sanity_check` override; the scanner's sweep mode misses channels 11–13 (use a fixed tune), and the target was on ch13.

### Live evil-twin attack test (5 November)

Validation of the second rule on a two-radio setup. The real "Redmi 9" hotspot and a Heltec ESP32-S3 (new `tools/esp32_evil_twin/`, a low-power softAP cloning the hotspot's BSSID via `esp_wifi_set_mac`) both beaconed as `fa:fa:99:c6:4b:c1` on ch13. The monitor raised `rf_identity_two_transmitters` at Medium/Medium: two carrier offsets under one BSSID, −2.215 ppm (phone) and −0.116 ppm (ESP), gap 2.10 ppm, interleaved. Recording in `data/rf_fingerprint/2026-11-05/eviltwin.ndjson`.

### SDR CFO-match blind-spot boundary (5 November)

Measured how closely an attacker must match an AP's carrier offset to evade `rf_forged_disconnect`. Built `tools/wifi_tx_gen.cpp`, a legacy-802.11a deauth waveform generator self-validated against our own receiver, and attempted a live HackRF transmission; the live decode was defeated by near-field levels (too weak = no decode; full power = X310 saturation), so the boundary was measured data-driven from the real deauth-test recordings (real beacons + real forged deauths, attacker CFO recentered to AP+delta, exact rule thresholds; validated against the real incident). Result: detection boundary ≈ 0.25 ppm (~600 Hz at 2.4 GHz) — an SDR within ~0.25 ppm of the AP's offset evades the rule. Notes in `data/rf_fingerprint/2026-11-05/`.
